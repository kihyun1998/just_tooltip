# Visibility scheduling

## What it is

Decides *when* a show or hide is requested, in wall-clock time: `waitDuration` before showing,
`showDuration` auto-hide, and the [Hover Bridge](../../../GLOSSARY.md#hover-bridge) grace window.
It emits requests; it never shows anything. Vocabulary: [Visibility Scheduler](../../../GLOSSARY.md#visibility-scheduler).

## Governing decisions

- [ADR-0001](../../adr/0001-scheduler-separates-intent-from-render-state.md) — timing and intent only; no render state, no visibility state, config passed per call.
- [ADR-0012](../../adr/0012-wait-duration-delays-only-a-hidden-tooltip.md) — `waitDuration` delays only a hidden tooltip; a trigger returning while it is shown or fading out turns it back at once (maintainer, 2026-11-12); a controller show cancels a hover show still waiting.
- [ADR-0012, amended 2026-11-16](../../adr/0012-wait-duration-delays-only-a-hidden-tooltip.md#amendment--2026-11-16-the-body-of-an-interactive-tooltip-holds-it-as-its-child-does) — the body of an interactive tooltip holds it as its child does: its pause and resume pair up whatever `enableHover` is (#147), and no show starts `showDuration` while the pointer rests on it (#133), and the pointer entering it during a fade-out turns the fade back as entering the child would (#134) (maintainer, 2026-11-16).
- [ADR-0012, #149 and #154](../../adr/0012-wait-duration-delays-only-a-hidden-tooltip.md) — a pointer resting on the body, entered with `enableHover` on, keeps another trigger leaving from arming the Hover Bridge; a body entered with `enableHover` off cancels no bridge (maintainer, 2026-11-17).

## Design model

From ADR-0001, confirmed in source:

- Holds three timers and nothing else; `isShown`, whether the pointer rests on the body (`pointerOnBody`) and `TooltipScheduleConfig` arrive with the calls that read them. `dispose`/`reset` → `_cancelAll` is the one leak-safety point.
- **The body's pause is a fact, not a missing timer** (maintainer, 2026-11-16, #133). `startAutoHide` arms nothing while `pointerOnBody`; `onTooltipExit` starts it with `pointerOnBody: false`. Every caller that starts the countdown passes it — `onChildEnter`, `onTap`, `onProgrammaticShow` — so no show made while the pointer rests on the body restarts it. Before, the pause was only `onTooltipEnter` cancelling the timer, and the next show re-armed it: a second `controller.show()` with the pointer on the body hid the tooltip under it 800 ms later (measured on fd0a9be), and keyboard focus arriving by Tab on a controller-shown tooltip did the same through `onChildEnter` (measured in #133's run; the conductor's two setups did not reproduce it). `onTap`'s value is `true` only in the frame a hidden tooltip's body is still in the tree, since a tap shows only a hidden tooltip; no test drives it. The fact itself is `_JustTooltipState._pointerOnBody`, set and cleared by the body's `MouseRegion` edges, and cleared when that region leaves the tree (`_OnDispose` around it), since a region removed from the tree sends no `onExit` (`RenderMouseRegion.detach` sets `validForMouseTracker` false, Flutter 3.47.1). It follows the region, not the show: `_endShow` hides the overlay, but the body stays in the render tree until the next frame, and a hide and a show in one frame keep the same region under the pointer, which sends no new `onEnter`. A first cut cleared it in `_endShow`, and both windows broke it, each measured in #133's check with a zero-length fade: the pointer entering the body after `controller.hide()` and before the next pump set the fact with no exit to follow, and the next show never started its countdown (still shown 2 s later, `[show, hide, show]`; fd0a9be hid it); `controller.hide(); controller.show();` with the pointer resting on the body cleared the fact under the pointer, and the countdown ran (gone within 800 ms). Kept in the State rather than as a flag in the scheduler: the scheduler would learn the body's end only from `reset()`, which also runs under a resting pointer (`_dismissByUser` on Escape, the suppression and covered hides), so a fade turned back afterwards would count down under the pointer — read, not run.
- `onChildEnter` arms the `waitDuration` timer only when `isShown` is false; a shown tooltip — fading out counts, since `_isShowing` stays true until the fade reaches `dismissed` — is asked to show at once, which turns the fade back (ADR-0012). This matches Flutter's `RawTooltipState._scheduleShowTooltip`, which delays only when its controller is dismissed. The timer therefore never outlives a fade it could have caught: before, a hover returning mid-fade waited past the fade's end, and the timer opened a tooltip with no show intent that the pointer leaving did not hide (#106).
- **The wait's end goes through the State** (maintainer, 2026-11-17, #155, a judgement, theirs to reverse). The `waitDuration` timer only emits `onWaitElapsed`; the State answers with `showAfterWait`, passing `_scheduleConfig` and `_pointerOnBody` as they are then (`_onWaitElapsed`), and the show and the countdown start from those. Every config field the fired show reads — today only `showDuration`, through `startAutoHide` — and `pointerOnBody` therefore come from the moment of firing. Before, `onChildEnter` armed `Timer(wait, () => _emitShow(config, …))` over the config of when the wait began: `showDuration` set during the wait started no countdown, `onChildExit` then left the hide to it, and the tooltip stayed shown for good; cleared during the wait, the old value closed a tooltip the pointer still rested on (both red on 42f99ef in `test/just_tooltip_live_config_test.dart`, group *changed while a show waits out waitDuration*, by hover and by keyboard focus). The issue held that a fix needed ADR-0001 changed, assuming the timer would have to read the config itself. It does not: the scheduler still keeps no config, only its timer handles, and config still arrives per call; the firing is one more call, made by the State. Rejected, shown to the maintainer: the scheduler taking a config reader at construction (amends ADR-0001 and ties it to the State's widget), and re-arming a pending wait on a config change (needs the wait's start time as scheduler state). What cancels the wait is unchanged, since the timer handle is the same: `onChildExit` (a reconcile that drops show intent), `onTap`, `onProgrammaticShow`, and `reset` (cover, clip-away, Escape, `controller.hide()`) or `dispose`. The scheduler tests count `onWaitElapsed` for `onChildExit`, `onTap`, `onProgrammaticShow`, `reset` and `dispose`, and each goes red with its cancel removed (measured by #155's run; `onTap`'s by its conductor, whose full suite stayed green without that cancel until *a tap during waitDuration cancels the wait* was added). A `waitDuration` change during the wait does not change it ([live-configuration](live-configuration.md)).
- Two hide requests (#129): `onHide` for a hide the trigger leaving asks for — a child or body exit without the bridge, the bridge running out — and `onClose` for one it did not cause — a tap on a shown tooltip, the auto-hide. `onClose` defaults to `onHide`, so a scheduler built with one callback behaves as before. The widget needs them apart because only the second is an explicit close of an evicted tooltip ([show-intent](show-intent.md)); pinned in `test/tooltip_visibility_scheduler_test.dart`.
- `onProgrammaticShow` (a controller show) cancels a hover show still waiting out `waitDuration` and an armed bridge, and restarts the auto-hide. Left armed, the timer fired after the controller's show: a tooltip the controller or the registry had hidden meanwhile opened with no show intent, a controller hide whose fade outlasted the wait was turned back, and `showDuration` counted from the end of the wait (measured, #106's lens). A bridge left armed hid the shown tooltip when it fired: a `show()` 40 ms after the pointer left a hover-shown interactive tooltip was gone 800 ms later, with `onShow` then `onHide`, and the same after keyboard focus left (measured on 5b0b463, #131).
- A controller hide while a show waits out `waitDuration` cancels the wait through `reset()` (`hideTooltip` → `_dismissByUser`, #136). It does not go through `onClose`: the auto-hide timer survives a hide that does not reset the scheduler, so a left-over one can fire during a later wait. During a new trigger act's wait on an evicted tooltip `_closeOnRequest` does not take it as a close (#129's guard); during the wait a revival begins it does: `_closeOnRequest` cancels that wait through `_dismissByUser`, whose `reset()` stops the wait timer (ADR-0010 as amended by #143; [show-intent](show-intent.md)).
- `onChildExit` with `showDuration` set does nothing — the countdown owns hiding. Without it: `interactive` arms the bridge, otherwise hides at once (`_hideOnTriggerExit`). **With `hoverOnBody` it asks for no hide** (maintainer, 2026-11-17, #149): a pointer that entered the body with `enableHover` on rests there, and the body's exit asks for the hide through `onTooltipExit`, by the bridge or at once (#150). `_reconcileShowIntent` passes `_pointerOnBody && _bodyEnteredWithHover`. Besides keyboard focus leaving and `enableFocus` turned off, the pointer crossing from the child onto the body reaches it: the body's `onEnter` records the pointer before `_flushReconcile` derives the child's `onExit` from the same batch, so that crossing arms no bridge where it used to arm one and cancel it at once (same result on screen). Without the flush only the turn-back test *turns back an auto-hide fade, and pauses showDuration* goes red (measured by #149's run), so the flush now serves the turn-back decision rather than the bridge. Before #149 focus leaving under a resting pointer hid the tooltip after the bridge, where resting on the child kept it. Show intent is not touched: the hold is a scheduler input, as the body's pause of the countdown is, and the hides that bypass the scheduler (eviction, Escape, `controller.hide()`, clip-away, Covered) still hide a held tooltip (pinned in `test/just_tooltip_body_test.dart`), and so does content loss (read, not pinned). Nesting suppression does not (Known holes). A body that appears under a still pointer holds too (maintainer, 2026-11-17, ADR-0012; pinned).
- **A `showDuration` change applies at once** (maintainer, 2026-11-17, #151; the cases are the implementer's derivations, ADR-0012). `onShowDurationChange`, called by `didUpdateWidget` on a shown tooltip not fading out, runs `startAutoHide` with the new value: set or changed, the countdown restarts from the change, paused while `pointerOnBody`; cleared, it is cancelled. Cleared, a trigger exit the countdown held asks for its hide: with the pointer off the body through `_hideOnTriggerExit` at once, with it on the body at the body's exit, where `onTooltipExit` with `exitLeftToCountdown` and no `showDuration` takes `_hideOnTriggerExit` too, whatever `enableHover` is. The fact is `_JustTooltipState._exitLeftToCountdown`: set when show intent drops with `showDuration` set while shown, and when the body's exit is a hover exit (`enableHover` or `_bodyEnteredWithHover`) with `showDuration` set, focus not holding the tooltip; cleared when show intent comes back, by `showTooltip`, and when a show opens. It lasts the rest of the show, so a body re-entered during the bridge it arms asks again on its exit. Before #151 the countdown started only on a show: set on a tooltip shown with none, `onChildExit` left the hide to a countdown that never ran, and the tooltip stayed shown for good once the trigger left (red on 8e9a797, `test/just_tooltip_live_config_test.dart`). The change is skipped during a fade-out: a countdown armed then outlived the fade and, firing on an evicted tooltip waiting for its revival, marked it dismissed (measured: the mutation kept the tooltip from coming back).
- The bridge (`_hoverBridgeDelay`, 100 ms) is armed on leaving the child or the body and cancelled by `onTooltipEnter` (with `enableHover` on) **and** `onChildEnter`, and by every show: `onTap` and `onProgrammaticShow` as well.
- `onTooltipEnter` pauses auto-hide whatever `enableHover` is (#147), and cancels the bridge only with `enableHover` on (maintainer, 2026-11-17, #154): a bridge armed before an entry made with hover off — by the child's exit when `enableHover` turned off under the pointer, or by keyboard focus leaving — runs out under the pointer. The entry used to cancel it, and the exit of a body entered with hover off arms nothing, so the tooltip stayed shown for good (reproduced on bb75909: still shown 2 s after the pointer left, `[show]`). With `turnBack`, which the State decides ([show-intent](show-intent.md), #134), it also requests a show, which turns a fade-out back. `onTooltipExit` restarts the auto-hide whatever `enableHover` and `interactive` are now. With no `showDuration` and `enableHover` on, it arms the bridge, or, with `interactive` off, requests the hide at once through `onHide`, as `onChildExit` does (maintainer, 2026-11-16, #148). That exit arrives with `interactive` off only when `interactive` was turned off under a resting pointer: the bridge it used to skip was the only hide left, since show intent had dropped at the child's exit, and a hover-shown tooltip stayed shown for good (measured on fd0a9be and ada5e3e: shown 3 s after the change, and 1 s after the pointer left). Hiding after the bridge was shown to the maintainer and not chosen. Keyboard focus still holds it: the body's `onExit` in `just_tooltip.dart` returns before calling `onTooltipExit` while keyboard focus holds the tooltip (`_heldOpenByFocus`, not merely focus inside; [show-intent](show-intent.md)) and there is no `showDuration`. The pause and its end pair up (maintainer, 2026-11-16, #147): the restart used to return early without `enableHover`, so a tooltip opened by a tap or `controller.show()` whose body the pointer crossed never auto-hid again (measured on fd0a9be: shown 2 s after the pointer crossed), and without `interactive`, which is read when the body's `onExit` arrives — `interactive` turned off under a resting pointer sends that exit after the next frame (measured on fd0a9be: shown 3 s later). The enter cannot arrive without `interactive`: the body sits in `IgnorePointer(ignoring: !interactive)`.
- **A body entered with hover on keeps its hover exit** (#150). With `enableHover` off it is still a hover exit when the pointer entered the body while `enableHover` was on (`enteredWithHover`), and it requests the hide at once; otherwise it does nothing (maintainer, 2026-11-17, #150; hiding at once rather than after the bridge is the implementer's derivation: the bridge serves a crossing onto a hover trigger, the child has no `MouseRegion` with hover off, and a body re-entered during the bridge would be entered with hover off, so its exit would arm nothing — ADR-0012). The entry fact is `_JustTooltipState._bodyEnteredWithHover`, set by the body's `onEnter` from the current `enableHover` and read by its `onExit`. It lives in the State, not the scheduler, because the scheduler keeps no state but its timers (ADR-0001). `enableHover` turning off sends the body no edge, since its region stays, so nothing happens at the toggle itself. Before #150 the exit armed nothing, and a tooltip with no show intent that the pointer rested on stayed shown for good (measured by #133's run on fd0a9be; red on bd8de03 in `test/just_tooltip_body_test.dart`).
- `onTap` toggles immediately, cancelling hover timers.

The 100 ms value was taken from Flutter's tooltip exit duration (maintainer, 2026-10-23).

## Reference behaviour

- Flutter 3.41.9 `widgets/raw_tooltip.dart` — `RawTooltip` defaults `dismissDelay` to 100 ms and `material/tooltip.dart` `_defaultExitDuration` is 100 ms: the source of `_hoverBridgeDelay`. `RawTooltipState._scheduleShowTooltip` / `_scheduleDismissTooltip` are the equivalent timing layer. Flutter 3.47.1: one `_timer` carries both the show delay and the dismiss delay (`Timer(withDelay, _controller.reverse)`), and `ensureTooltipVisible`, its programmatic show, cancels it first — the same rule `onProgrammaticShow` follows for the two timers here.

## Blast radius

- [visibility-lifecycle](visibility-lifecycle.md) — it acts on the requests; a request emitted while fading out relies on the reverse-catch in `_show`.
- [show-intent](show-intent.md) — unit tests here feed events in synchronous order; a change in what order is assumed breaks `interactive` only at the widget seam (ADR-0003, last consequence).
- [programmatic-control](programmatic-control.md) — `showTooltip` calls `onProgrammaticShow`; a programmatic show has no other path into these timers.
- [public-surface](public-surface.md) — `waitDuration`, `showDuration`, `interactive` are documented promises.

## Known holes / open

- `isShown` is passed to `onChildExit` / `onTooltipExit` but neither reads it; `onChildEnter` and `onTap` do.
- **Nesting suppression does not hide a tooltip the body holds** (maintainer's call, 2026-11-17, recorded rather than fixed; ADR-0012). Suppression hides only through show intent's transition to `false` in `_reconcileShowIntent`; a tooltip shown with no show intent left — held by a resting pointer on its body after keyboard focus left (#149), or hover-shown with the pointer crossed onto its body (before #149 too) — has none to drop, so a nested tooltip in a registry of its own shows beside it, against *innermost wins* (ADR-0003). Measured by #149's run: `[show A, show D]` for both paths; bb75909 hid the focus path's A after the bridge, before D showed. In one registry D's show evicts A.
