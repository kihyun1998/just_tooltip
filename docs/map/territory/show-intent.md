# Show intent

## What it is

Turns the trigger edges on the child — `MouseRegion` enter/exit, focus moving — into one derived
boolean, and hands only its transitions to the scheduler; and dismisses a shown tooltip on
Escape. It is the first link of the [visibility chain](visibility-chain.md). Called hover intent
until ADR-0010. Vocabulary: [Show Intent](../../../GLOSSARY.md#show-intent),
[Dismissal](../../../GLOSSARY.md#dismissal).

## Governing decisions

- [ADR-0003](../../adr/0003-nesting-suppression-gates-a-derived-hover-intent.md) — introduces the pointer-facts layer and the microtask coalescing.
- [ADR-0004](../../adr/0004-content-gates-showing-suppressing-and-hiding.md) — adds `hasContent` as the fourth term.
- [ADR-0011](../../adr/0011-a-covered-tooltip-hides.md) — adds [Covered](../../../GLOSSARY.md#covered) as a gate; a covered hide is not a dismissal (maintainer, 2026-11-08).
- [ADR-0010](../../adr/0010-a-keyboard-user-can-show-and-dismiss-a-tooltip.md) — adds the keyboard-focus fact (`enableFocus`, opt-in; key-arrived focus only; the pointer wins; an evicted tooltip comes back) and dismissal on Escape; WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.13 is the yardstick (maintainer, 2026-10-26).
- [ADR-0010, amended by #116](../../adr/0010-a-keyboard-user-can-show-and-dismiss-a-tooltip.md) — before its revival an evicted tooltip gains show intent only on a new trigger act; every other re-derivation waits (maintainer, 2026-11-12).
- [ADR-0012](../../adr/0012-wait-duration-delays-only-a-hidden-tooltip.md) — a trigger returning while the tooltip is shown or fading out skips `waitDuration` (maintainer, 2026-11-12).

## Design model

From the records, confirmed in source:

- `wants = _triggerInside && !_suppressed && _hasContent && !_dismissed && !_covered && !waiting` (`_reconcileShowIntent`), where `waiting = !_showIntent && !act && _registry.isEvicted(this)` (below, *New trigger act*) and `_triggerInside = (enableHover && _pointerInside) || (enableFocus && _keyboardFocusInside)`; `if (wants == _showIntent) return` — only transitions reach the scheduler.
- **The focus fact.** `_holdsFocus` walks `FocusManager.primaryFocus.context`'s ancestors up to this State's element, so focus in the child *or* in the tooltip's content counts (the overlay child sits in the tooltip's element subtree, ADR-0005). No widget is put around the child: a first cut wrapped it in a `Focus`, and #92's review measured that `Focus.of(context)` inside every consumer's child then returned the tooltip's node instead of theirs, `enableFocus` or not. `_onFocusChanged` (a `FocusManager` listener, only while `enableFocus`) sets `_keyboardFocusInside` when focus arrives inside while one of `_traversalKeys` (Tab, the arrow keys) is in `HardwareKeyboard.logicalKeysPressed` — a Tab is still down when the traversal it triggered lands, a focus change is applied in a microtask before the key-up — and keeps it while focus moves about inside. Any pressed key was not enough (#92's second review, measured): a Shift-click counted, and so did the focus a dialog closed by Escape gives back a frame later while Escape is still down. `highlightMode` was the first choice and was dropped: a desktop mouse click leaves it `traditional` (measured).
- **Suppression has two kinds.** `_syncSuppression` claims ancestors in `_suppressors` for the pointer and in `_focusSuppressors` for keyboard focus; `_suppressed` counts a focus suppressor only while the pointer is not inside this tooltip's child, so a pointer hovering an outer child elsewhere wins (ADR-0010). A dismissed tooltip keeps suppressing: its outer one does not pop up in its place.
- **Eviction.** An evicted tooltip comes back only once no tooltip in its own registry holds the place (ADR-0010 as amended by #113; maintainer, 2026-11-11). The record lives in the [registry](tooltip-registry.md): `TooltipRegistry.show` moves the tooltip holding the place into its evicted set and calls its `dismissTooltip`, which drops `_showIntent` and hides; when the holder's show ends — its fade reaches `dismissed`, or it leaves the tree while shown or fading out — `_endShow`'s `remove` finds it holding the place and calls `reviveTooltip` on every evicted one, which reconciles it, so a trigger still inside shows it again (#111 put both paths through `_endShow`). In a chain — A hover-shown, D's `show()` evicts A, B's `show()` evicts D — A comes back when B hides, not when D's fade ends; a show ending in another registry revives nothing (both pinned in `test/just_tooltip_eviction_test.dart`, red on efd4042 where one app-wide set was re-derived by any show ending). A tooltip fading out because it was evicted does not hold the place, so when the holder leaves the tree during that fade the evicted one turns its fade back and calls no second `onShow` (pinned in `test/just_tooltip_teardown_test.dart`). A tooltip torn down while not holding the place revives nothing: with that guard removed, removing a tooltip that never showed brought A back over the B that evicted it, and A dismissed B (measured by mutation, #111 and again here). The evicted one's own hide does not clear its entry; `dispose` drops it (`forget`). A show that turns back its fade — a hover or a `controller.show()` — takes the registry back and leaves the evicted set, dismissing the tooltip that evicted it (#101; for a hover, maintainer, 2026-11-08 — before, both stayed shown). A tooltip whose registry changes moves at once (#114; maintainer, 2026-11-11): leaving a registry where it held the place revives the tooltips it evicted there, and an evicted tooltip waits in its new registry. `reviveTooltip` reconciles in a microtask, and only if by then no other tooltip holds the place in the tooltip's registry; otherwise it waits again ([tooltip-registry](tooltip-registry.md)).
- **New trigger act** (ADR-0010 as amended by #116; maintainer, 2026-11-12). Until the revival, the evicted tooltip's own re-derivations do not show it: `waiting` holds while the registry still records it (`isEvicted`), unless the reconcile carries a new act or show intent is already set. Before #116 they did: content regained, a `GlobalKey` move with focus inside and a nested release each found the trigger inside, `_show` ran, `_registry.show` took the place and dismissed the holder (measured on 78a9c21). `_pointerAct` and `_focusAct` are set by the act-producing edges and read and cleared at the top of `_reconcileShowIntent`, where each counts only while its trigger is still inside (`enableHover && _pointerInside`, `enableFocus && _keyboardFocusInside`); a reconcile coalesced from an act and other changes counts as an act. The edges: the child's `onEnter` when `_pointerMoved` (`_pointerAct`); `_onFocusChanged` when key-arrived focus begins (`_focusAct`); and a descendant's release passed `moved: true` — from its `onExit` when `_pointerMoved`, and from its `_onFocusChanged` — which `_claimAncestors` turns into the ancestor's act of the released kind only when the ancestor was `_suppressed` before the release. So the pointer going from a nested tooltip straight out of the outer one, or focus leaving a nested tooltip whose suppression the pointer on the outer one overrode, is not an act; the first cut counted every moved release, and the review and the completeness read measured an evicted A held by focus coming back over its holder that way (pinned). A tap and `controller.show()` call `_show` directly and never pass the gate. What waits: content (`didUpdateWidget`), `enableHover`/`enableFocus` changes, `activate`, uncovering (`_syncCovered`), and a release from `_releaseAncestors` (the descendant leaving the tree or moving) or from the descendant's own `didUpdateWidget`. `_pointerMoved` is `schedulerPhase != postFrameCallbacks`: `MouseTracker` reports an edge in two ways, with a pointer event (`updateWithEvent`) and after a frame for the tree changing under a resting pointer (`updateAllDevices`, from `RendererBinding._scheduleMouseTrackerUpdate`, read in Flutter 3.47.1). A region mounted under a resting pointer gets `onEnter` that way, and a child shifted from under it `onExit` (both measured by probe), so `enableHover` turned on and a dialog popped over the child are not acts; that cut, the reading of a release as an act, the claim-is-not-an-act rule and keeping an act's show intent through `waitDuration` are the implementer's derivations (ADR-0010). The `!_showIntent` term matters only during `waitDuration`: without it, a configuration change during the wait dropped the act's show intent and the tooltip never showed (mutation, pinned). The record needs no field in the State, because the registry already keeps it for exactly as long as the tooltip waits. Pinned in `test/just_tooltip_eviction_test.dart` (group *before its revival …*): every wait case red on 78a9c21, every act case green there and after.
- **Focus outlasts the hover policy.** Leaving the body does not arm the [Hover Bridge](../../../GLOSSARY.md#hover-bridge) while keyboard focus holds the tooltip (unless `showDuration` owns hiding): the bridge hides without asking show intent, and a mouse crossing a focus-shown body hid it while focus stayed (measured). When the tooltip reaches `dismissed` by any path other than its trigger leaving — a tap, the auto-hide, a controller — with `_showIntent` still set, `_onAnimationStatus` drops it and sets `_dismissed = _triggerInside`, so the state says what the screen shows and the tooltip waits for its trigger to return. No hover show is waiting out `waitDuration` by then: a returning trigger skips the wait while the tooltip is shown, and a controller show cancels one already waiting ([every-show-cancels-the-waiting-hover-show](../invariant/every-show-cancels-the-waiting-hover-show.md)). Before #106 one could be, and when it fired it opened the tooltip with no show intent, which the trigger leaving did not hide.
- **Dismissal.** `_shown` (static) lists shown tooltips in show order — a reverse-catch re-tracks — and `_dismissTarget` is the last one not already reversing. `_dismissByUser` sets `_dismissed = _triggerInside`, drops `_showIntent`, resets the scheduler and hides; the reconcile clears `_dismissed` once no trigger is inside. A move inside the child sends no edge and never re-derives, so the flag matters only where something else reconciles, such as a nested tooltip releasing it (pinned). `_pending` holds tooltips with show intent not yet on screen; Escape with nothing shown dismisses them. The `HardwareKeyboard` handler is added while `_shown` or `_pending` is non-empty (`_syncKeyHandler`).
- **Two Escape paths, because only one can consume** (measured on Flutter 3.41.9, #92). `KeyEventManager` hands a key to every `HardwareKeyboard` handler *and* to the focus system's `Shortcuts`, whatever the handlers return, so a handler cannot keep a dialog's `DismissIntent` from closing it. `_handleKey` therefore closes a target that does not hold focus itself — the hover case, where the dialog closes too — and leaves one that does in `_deferred`, with a microtask to close it after the press. `_DismissTooltipAction`, the `DismissIntent` action in an `Actions` around the whole tooltip (child and content), is on the focus path and nearer than the route's: it closes `_deferred` and consumes. A nearer enabled action inside the tooltip (a `DropdownMenu`'s) takes the key instead; the microtask then closes the tooltip. Either way one press closes one tooltip: the handler runs first and does one or the other.
- **Forwarding.** With nothing deferred the action hands the intent to the nearest `DismissIntent` action above the tooltip, found by walking `Actions` widgets (no dependency is registered, unlike `Actions.maybeFind`) and called with the focused context when it is a `ContextAction`. A nearer `Actions` for an intent shadows the ancestor's even while disabled (measured), so returning disabled would have kept every dialog around a tooltip from closing on Escape.
- **Covered** (ADR-0011). `_covered` reads `_route.isCurrent` at the moment it is asked; `_RouteStatus`, a child that depends on `ModalRoute.of`, only reports the route so `_updateCovered` can act on a change. On covering, `_syncCovered` drops `_showIntent` and `_pending` and resets the scheduler at once, then hides after the frame, so `_onAnimationStatus` finds no show intent and sets no `_dismissed` — with a zero-length fade the hide ends synchronously, and dropping show intent after it would have marked the tooltip dismissed (pinned by the zero-duration case in `test/just_tooltip_covered_test.dart`). On uncovering it reconciles only when a trigger is inside, since otherwise the reconcile is a no-op on every tooltip of the route. The gate in `wants` matters for a reconcile that runs while covered (a configuration change): without it `_showIntent` turns true, `_show` refuses, and the tooltip never reopens.
- `activate` re-runs `_syncSuppression` and the reconcile after `_readScope`: a reparented tooltip still holding focus shows again and claims its new ancestors — unless it is evicted and another tooltip holds the place, where it waits for the revival (#116).
- Edge handlers only record facts (`_pointerInside`, `_anchorCandidate`) and call `_syncSuppression` synchronously, then `_scheduleReconcile`. The reconcile is coalesced into one microtask because Flutter dispatches every `onExit` then every `onEnter` synchronously for one move.
- One escape hatch: the tooltip body's `onEnter` calls `_flushReconcile` before `onTooltipEnter`, so the child's deferred exit has armed the bridge before the body cancels it.
- A transition to `false` caused by suppression or lost content is not a hover exit: `_scheduler.reset()` + `_hide()`, bypassing `onChildExit`'s `showDuration` policy.
- `onExit` clears `_anchorCandidate` so a later programmatic show cannot anchor at a cursor that has gone.
- A non-interactive body sits in `IgnorePointer(ignoring: !interactive)`, so it never takes the pointer from its child. Placement can lay the body over the child — one too large for either side is clamped onto it — and an opaque body there made the child report `onExit`, the tooltip hide at once, the child uncover and the tooltip show again: shown on every other 50 ms sample in a 390-wide view with a 300-wide `left` body (#65). An interactive body keeps taking the pointer, because the bridge it cancels on `onEnter` is what keeps it shown. Flutter's `Tooltip` makes the same split (`ignorePointer ?? message != null`, Flutter 3.47.1 `material/tooltip.dart`). Cost: a tap on a non-interactive body reaches the widget beneath it.

Input: pointer, focus and key events; four retained bools and one offset per tooltip, static sets as large as the tooltips shown or pending at once, and per registry a set as large as the tooltips it has evicted.

## Reference behaviour

- Flutter 3.41.9 `widgets/raw_tooltip.dart` — `RawTooltipState._handleMouseEnter` / `_handleMouseExit` keep a set of hovering devices (`_activeHoveringPointerDevices`) rather than a single edge, and rely on the documented "all `onExit` before any `onEnter`" order in a comment. This package does not rely on that order; it coalesces instead (ADR-0003 calls the order undocumented).
- Flutter 3.41.9 `widgets/raw_tooltip.dart`, `material/tooltip.dart` — no focus trigger and no Escape handling; this territory goes past them on purpose (ADR-0010). `services/hardware_keyboard.dart` `KeyEventManager.handleKeyData` — both the `HardwareKeyboard` handlers and `keyMessageHandler` run for every key; `widgets/actions.dart` `Actions.maybeInvoke` — a suitable action found disabled stops the search; `widgets/focus_manager.dart` — `FocusManager` notifies its listeners when `primaryFocus` changes, and `_HighlightModeManager` switches to touch only for touch and stylus pointers.

## Blast radius

- [visibility-scheduling](visibility-scheduling.md) — it assumes it is fed transitions in "child, then tooltip" order; the flush is what keeps that true.
- [nesting-suppression](nesting-suppression.md) — `_suppressed` is a term of the formula, and the claim must stay synchronous while the reconcile is deferred.
- [content](content.md) — `_hasContent` is a term; content loss takes the immediate-hide branch.
- [target-resolution](target-resolution.md) — `_anchorCandidate` recorded here is what a pointer anchor freezes.
- [tap-trigger](tap-trigger.md) — the tap path writes `_anchorCandidate` too and bypasses show intent entirely; a tap still opens a dismissed tooltip.
- [visibility-lifecycle](visibility-lifecycle.md) — `_show` tracks the tooltip in `_shown`; `_endShow`, reached from the dismissed animation status and from `_hideImmediate`, untracks it and leaves the registry, which revives the evicted tooltips.
- [tooltip-registry](tooltip-registry.md) — the registry records the evicted tooltips and decides when they are revived; `_shown` keeps a second, app-wide record of what is shown.
- [semantic-tooltip](semantic-tooltip.md) — nothing may be put around the child that adds a semantics node, or the child's node splits.

## Known holes / open

- Multi-pointer: one `_pointerInside` bool per tooltip, where `RawTooltip` tracks a device set. Two mice are not modelled; nothing records whether that is deliberate.
- Tests that inspect state between `onEnter` and the microtask see the stale value (ADR-0003, consequences) — any new test here must pump.
- Whether the key goes on to the platform (`_DismissTooltipAction.consumesKey`, and its `isEnabled` claiming the deferred press when no action encloses the tooltip) is invisible to a widget test; both mutations stay green.
- The state cleanup at `dismissed` (`_showIntent` still set) changes nothing a widget test sees — the next show still needs the trigger to return — but without it the tooltip stays in `_pending` and the keyboard handler stays installed; its mutation stays green (measured again with #106's tests, which do not see it either).
- Forwarding calls the enclosing action directly, so an ancestor `Actions(dispatcher: …)` does not see an Escape that passes through a tooltip (read, not run).
- An evicted tooltip whose child is scrolled, resized or laid out under a resting pointer waits for the revival: the edge comes after a frame and is not a new act (implementer's derivation, #116; the maintainer was not shown this case). A wheel scroll is the user's act but moves no pointer.
- When several evicted tooltips are revived together, the order they were evicted in decides which one ends up shown: hover-held A and focus-held F both evicted, D hides → F shows, and A is evicted again (measured by the #113 lens). Nothing records which should win; ADR-0010's "the pointer wins" covers nesting only.
- Several views share `_shown` and one keyboard handler, so an Escape typed in one view closes a hovered tooltip in another (ADR-0010, accepted; read, not run).
