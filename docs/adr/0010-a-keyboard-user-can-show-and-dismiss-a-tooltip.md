# 0010 — A keyboard user can show and dismiss a tooltip

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-26
- **Context issue:** [#92](https://github.com/kihyun1998/just_tooltip/issues/92) (a focused child does not show its tooltip, and nothing dismisses it with Escape)
- **Amends:** [0003 — Nesting suppression gates a derived hover intent](0003-nesting-suppression-gates-a-derived-hover-intent.md): hover intent becomes [Show Intent](../../GLOSSARY.md#show-intent), with a focus fact and a dismissal term.

## Context

A tooltip showed on hover, on tap and by `controller.show()`, never on focus, and no key closed it. Measured on Flutter 3.41.9 (#92's triage): a `TextButton` child given focus by `requestFocus()` showed nothing; a shown tooltip stayed open through Escape. Flutter's own `Tooltip` does not show on focus either, and neither `raw_tooltip.dart` nor `material/tooltip.dart` handles Escape, so this goes past the prior art and rests on its own reasons.

The yardstick is WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.13, Content on Hover or Focus (w3.org/TR/WCAG21, read 2026-10-26). For content that pointer hover or keyboard focus makes visible, it asks that the content be **dismissible** "without moving pointer hover or keyboard focus", **hoverable**, and **persistent** "until the hover or focus trigger is removed, the user dismisses it, or its information is no longer valid". Its Note 2 names custom tooltips.

## Decision

Maintainer's calls (2026-10-26, grilling on #92's triage):

- **Focus is a core trigger, opt-in: `enableFocus: bool = false`**, beside `enableHover`. A consumer could already call `controller.show()` from `Focus(onFocusChange:)`, but that path bypasses nesting suppression (a programmatic show is not suppressed, ADR-0003) and the trigger-removal hide, which only a core trigger joins. Off by default, so no consumer's keyboard behaviour changes. Chosen over on-by-default and over leaving it to consumers.
- **Focus joins hover in one derived state, renamed Show Intent.** The focus fact sits beside the pointer fact; `waitDuration`, nesting suppression, the content gate and the hide on losing the trigger apply to both from one place. Chosen over a separate path like tap (ADR-0006), which would need suppression rebuilt for it.
- **Only keyboard focus counts** — focus that arrives inside the tooltip while a focus-moving key (Tab, an arrow key) is down; a click, a tap, `requestFocus()`, a Shift-click or focus a closing route gives back does not. Grilling first chose `FocusManager.highlightMode == traditional`, the condition for a focus ring; the completeness read then measured that a mouse click on desktop leaves that mode on, so a click opened the tooltip and kept it until focus left. Shown that, the maintainer chose key-arrived focus (2026-10-26, #92's check).
- **The pointer wins over focus.** A nested tooltip holding focus suppresses its ancestors only while the pointer is not inside them, so a pointer hovering the outer child elsewhere shows the outer tooltip. Measured before the call: the focused inner one kept the outer one silent (maintainer, 2026-10-26, #92's check).
- **A tooltip hidden by the registry comes back.** One whose trigger is still inside when another tooltip takes its place re-derives once no tooltip in its own registry holds the place. Hover never needed it — the pointer cannot be in two children — but focus and the pointer can, and a focus-held tooltip stayed gone (measured; maintainer, 2026-10-26, #92's check).
  - **Amended 2026-11-11 (#113), the maintainer's call.** This read "re-derives when that one hides", and the code re-derived every evicted tooltip whenever any tooltip's show ended. Shown two widget-test repros on efd4042 (Flutter 3.47.1): a chain — A hover-shown, D's `show()` evicts A, B's `show()` evicts D, and when D's fade ends A comes back and dismisses B — and a cross-registry case — A and B in one registry, C in another, B evicts A, and `c.hide()` brings A back over B. The alternative was the literal reading: re-derive when the tooltip that took the place hides, which fixes the cross-registry case and keeps the chain behaviour. The maintainer chose "only once no tooltip in its own registry holds the place": in the chain A comes back when B hides, and a show ending in another registry brings nothing back. A tooltip fading out because it was evicted does not hold the place. This is a judgement, not a derivation; it is the maintainer's to reverse. The call did not cover a tooltip whose registry changes while shown (#114): the maintainer was not shown that this change makes one of its paths worse (the old registry's evicted tooltips wait for another show there to end), so that cost was untested by them, not accepted. #114 then moved a tooltip to its new registry at once (ADR-0007, amended), which closes that path.
  - **Amended 2026-11-12 (#116), the maintainer's call (triage of #116).** #113's rule held for the registry's own revival, but the evicted tooltip's own re-derivations were not gated: one that found its trigger still inside showed it, took the place and dismissed the holder. Measured on 78a9c21 (Flutter 3.47.1, widget tests, one shared registry): A's content lost and regained while evicted → A shown, D hidden; a focus-held F moved by a `GlobalKey` → F shown, H hidden with the pointer on H; the pointer moving from a nested tooltip back onto A → A shown, D hidden. The maintainer chose: while another tooltip holds the place, an evicted tooltip shows again **only on a new trigger act** — the pointer entering its child, or moving onto it from a nested tooltip that was suppressing it (a suppression released by the pointer or keyboard focus moving counts as an act, so the third repro is intended); keyboard focus arriving in it by a key; a tap on it; `controller.show()`. An act shows it as before: it takes the place and dismisses the holder. **Every other re-derivation waits** for the revival, with no visible change meanwhile: its content returning; a suppression released for another reason (the nested tooltip losing its content, leaving the tree, or having `enableHover`/`enableFocus` turned off); `activate` after a `GlobalKey` move with focus still inside; its route becoming current again; `enableHover` or `enableFocus` turned on. When the revival comes, a trigger still inside shows it. This is a judgement, not a derivation; it is the maintainer's to reverse. It did not cover which of several evicted tooltips wins when they are revived together.
    - **The implementer's derivations under that call.** (1), (2) and (4) were then shown to the maintainer with their costs and accepted (2026-11-12, #116's run); (3) was not shown and stays the implementer's. (1) "The pointer entering" is a `MouseRegion` edge the pointer's own movement caused. `MouseTracker` also reports edges after a frame, for the tree changing under a resting pointer: a region mounted there gets `onEnter` (so `enableHover` turned on does), a dialog popped from over the child gives one too, and a child moved out from under the pointer gets `onExit` (measured, Flutter 3.47.1). Those come in the post-frame phase and are not acts, which the listed waits for `enableHover` and the route need. The cost, accepted by the maintainer: a child scrolled, resized or laid out under a resting pointer, while evicted, waits for the revival too. (2) "Moving onto it from a nested tooltip that was suppressing it" is read as written: a release is an act only when the nested tooltip was suppressing the outer one and the trigger that moved is still inside the outer one afterwards. A release by the pointer leaving the outer one entirely, or by focus leaving a nested tooltip whose suppression the pointer already overrode, is not an act; the first cut counted every release by a moving trigger, and the review and the completeness read both measured an evicted A held by focus coming back, and dismissing the holder, when the pointer went from a nested tooltip straight out of A, where going out from A itself left A waiting. A nested tooltip claiming the outer one is never an act. (3) Show intent an act has set is not dropped by a later re-derivation that is not an act, such as a configuration change during `waitDuration`; it is dropped as before when a term of show intent turns false, and the tooltip then waits again. (4) An evicted tooltip held by both the pointer and focus, one of which leaves, waits: that re-derivation is not on the list of acts.
  - **Amended 2026-11-12 (#129), the maintainer's call (recorded on #126's run and on #129).** An explicit close of an evicted tooltip cancels its revival whenever it lands: closed while it waits — during the fade the eviction began or after it — it stays closed until its trigger leaves and returns, as a tooltip closed before the eviction already did (#117). Measured on dd1dc7d (Flutter 3.47.1, widget tests, one shared registry, A hover-shown, D's `controller.show()` evicting it, then `d.hide()`): `a.hide()` after the eviction's fade, a `TooltipTap.claim` tap 60 ms into it, and A's `showDuration` running out during it each brought A back; `a.hide()` during the fade already kept it closed since #126. Unchanged, as the maintainer listed: a tap on an evicted tooltip already hidden opens it (a new trigger act, #116 above); a close after the trigger has left changes nothing visible; `controller.show()` (#101) and the trigger returning (#106) during the fade still turn it back. This is a judgement, not a derivation; it is the maintainer's to reverse.
    - **The implementer's derivations under that call.** (1) was then shown to the maintainer with its costs (an invisible timer decides, and a focus-held tooltip may stay closed until focus returns by key) and accepted (2026-11-14, #129's run); (2)-(5) were not shown and stay the implementer's. (1) the auto-hide firing *after* the eviction's fade has ended cancels the revival too — the call says "whenever it lands", and the same timer firing either side of the fade's end would otherwise give opposite results; (2) `toggle()` never closes an evicted tooltip, since an evicted tooltip is always fading out or hidden and `toggle()` takes both as closed and shows it (#101); (3) a close counts only when the revival would otherwise show the tooltip: its trigger inside, nothing but the eviction holding it back, and no show intent a new trigger act already set — so a `hide()` on one that suppression had already taken out of wanting still lets the pointer moving back show it (#126), and a `hide()` while a new act's `waitDuration` runs changes nothing, as on a tooltip that is not evicted (for `controller.hide()`, superseded by #136 below: it now cancels that show and dismisses the tooltip; the auto-hide left over from an earlier show still changes nothing there); (4) the trigger leaving and the Hover Bridge are not explicit closes and do not count: the first cut let them through on the reading that the trigger is outside when they hide, and the review measured the bridge running out after the child slid back under a resting pointer marking the tooltip dismissed; (5) "whenever it lands" includes the moment after the holder's show has ended and before the revival has reconciled — a `hide()` from the holder's own `onHide`, say — where the registry no longer records the tooltip as evicted. A `controller.hide()` during `waitDuration` on a tooltip that is not evicted still does not cancel the waiting show; nobody has decided whether it should. (#136 decided it, below.)
- **Escape dismisses, on by default.** Dismissal only ever closes, so turning it on cannot break a layout. A hover-shown tooltip's child need not have focus, so the core listens to the keyboard while a tooltip is shown; a consumer has no focus to attach a shortcut to.
  - It closes **the most recently shown** visible tooltip, one per press. More than one can be visible: a host and a tooltip in its content (ADR-0007), or tooltips on separate registries.
  - It **consumes the key only where it can**: when focus is inside the closed tooltip's child, the close runs as the `DismissIntent` action on the focus path, so a dialog behind waits for the next press. A tooltip shown with focus elsewhere — a hover — is closed from a `HardwareKeyboard` handler, and that cannot consume: measured on Flutter 3.41.9, a handler returning `true` still let the dialog's Escape through, so both close. Patching `KeyEventManager.keyMessageHandler` would consume, but it can never be reverted, so it was rejected. Grilling chose "consume only when closed" before this was measured; shown the measurement, the maintainer chose this hybrid over a global handler alone and over the focus path alone, which would leave a hovered tooltip undismissable (2026-10-26, #92's confirm stop).
  - With nothing to close, the focus-path action forwards to the enclosing `DismissIntent` action: a nearer `Actions` for an intent shadows the ancestor's even while disabled (measured), so a tooltip around a dialog's button would otherwise keep the dialog from closing.
  - **One press closes one tooltip.** The keyboard handler runs before the focus path, so it either closes its target or leaves it to the focus path; it never does both. A widget inside the tooltip with its own enabled `DismissIntent` action — a `DropdownMenu` — takes the key first; the tooltip left to the focus path is then closed right after the press, without consuming.
  - With nothing shown, Escape **cancels a show still waiting** out `waitDuration`, and the tooltip stays dismissed until its trigger returns.
    - **Amended 2026-11-15 (#136), the maintainer's call (triage of #136).** `controller.hide()` made while a hover or focus show waits out `waitDuration` cancels that show and leaves the tooltip dismissed until its trigger leaves and returns, the same as Escape with nothing shown, and consistent with #129 (an explicit close always closes). Measured on 4f3db24 (Flutter 3.47.1, widget test): `waitDuration: 300 ms`, the pointer in, `a.hide()` at 100 ms — the tooltip showed when the wait ended; #129 had pinned that as current behaviour. This is a judgement, not a derivation; it is the maintainer's to reverse. The implementer's derivations under that call, not shown to the maintainer: (0) "while a show waits" covers every wait: an evicted tooltip a new trigger act is showing, which #129's derivation (3) had left unchanged, stays closed when the holder hides; and a tooltip the revival is showing, waiting out `waitDuration` again (ADR-0012), stays closed (it showed at the end of that wait on 4f3db24); (1) the cancel belongs to `controller.hide()` only, not to the scheduler's close (`onClose`): the auto-hide left over from an earlier show can fire while a new trigger act's show waits, and that timer belongs to the earlier show, so it still changes nothing there (#129's guard). The same left-over timer firing in the wait a revival begins changes nothing either, as on 4f3db24, though that timer belongs to the show being revived; whether #129's "whenever it lands" covers it is not decided; (2) a `hide()` on a tooltip that is shown, or fading out, closes it as before, so a `show()` turning that fade back leaves show intent set and the pointer leaving hides it; (3) a `show()` after a `hide()` that cancelled a wait opens the tooltip with no show intent and `_dismissed` set, so it stays when the pointer leaves, as after Escape and `show()`, or after `hide()` and `show()` once a fade has ended (#126) — where on 4f3db24 it hid when the pointer left.
  - It closes a tooltip **however it was opened**, `controller.show()` included: the user may always dismiss.
  - A dismissed tooltip stays closed **until its trigger leaves and returns** — the pointer leaves and re-enters the child, or focus leaves and comes back. A tap still toggles it.
    - **Amended 2026-11-15 (#127), the maintainer's calls (triage of #127, and a question from #127's run).** A dismissal is released only when the pointer, or keyboard focus, actually moves out of the child. Edges that come from the tree or the configuration changing under a still pointer do not release it — a route's barrier covering or uncovering the child, `enableHover` or the tap mode changing, a child moved or re-created under the pointer — the same distinction #116 drew for new trigger acts. A tooltip that was not dismissed behaves as before. Measured on 4f3db24 (Flutter 3.47.1, widget tests, `TooltipTap.claim`, the close's fade ended): a dialog pushed and popped over the child with the pointer resting → shown again; `enableHover` turned off and on → shown again. Asked during #127's run, with the measurement that the edges cannot tell it from a pointer that never moved (4f3db24 showed it again, the change left it closed, either `hideWhenCovered`): **the pointer leaving and returning while a dialog covers the child, the pop landing over it, leaves the tooltip dismissed; it shows after the pointer leaves and returns once after the pop.** Chosen over watching the pointer through a global pointer route while covered, as Flutter's `RawTooltip` does for tap-to-dismiss, and over comparing the pointer's position before and after, which would release a pointer that only moved within the child. Asked last, with measurements by #127's review and lens (the change at that point, against the tree without it): **a hold blocks only while its own trigger is reported inside** — the pointer's while `_pointerInside`, focus's while key-arrived focus is inside. Shown: with every hold blocking every trigger, a tooltip tap-closed, then the pointer leaving under a dialog and Tab after the pop; tap-closed, the pointer leaving while `enableHover` was off and Tab after it was back on; and Escape-closed, `enableFocus` turned off and on with focus inside and the pointer moving in — each stayed closed where the tree without the change showed it. Chosen over a hold that blocks every trigger until it is released. The two calls above stand: the edge after a frame that follows a pop or `enableHover` turned back on reports the pointer inside again, and its hold applies (pinned). These are judgements, not derivations; they are the maintainer's to reverse. The implementer's derivations under those calls, not shown to the maintainer: (1) the pointer entering the child by its own movement releases its hold too, since it was outside, so a pointer that left under the dialog and enters after the pop shows it; (2) keyboard focus leaving the tooltip releases its hold whatever moved it, a route taking focus included, since focus becomes the trigger again only by a key; focus that left while `enableFocus` was off releases it when `enableFocus` is turned back on; (3) a trigger that arrives while the tooltip is dismissed holds it too, which keeps "until no trigger is inside" for two triggers; (4) a show by show intent ends every hold, so a hold left by a trigger that was then turned off cannot hide a tooltip the other trigger showed once its trigger is back; (5) a child scrolled from under the resting pointer and back is "a child moved under the pointer" and leaves the tooltip dismissed; (6) a close still fading out when an edge after a frame, a `tap` change or `enableHover` turned off clears the pointer fact, or `enableFocus` turned off clears the focus fact, is settled first, while the trigger still counts, as #126 settles at every site that drops show intent — #127's lens and re-review measured the first cuts letting those bring the tooltip back during the fade; (7) a later close adds its holds to the ones an earlier close left, since replacing them released a pointer hold with no movement.
    - **Amended 2026-11-15 (#130), the maintainer's calls (triage of #130, and two questions from #130's run).** The hide [Target Tracking](../../GLOSSARY.md#target-tracking) makes when a child-anchored tooltip's child is clipped away entirely is not a dismissal, as a covered hide is not ([ADR-0011](0011-a-covered-tooltip-hides.md)). When the child is visible again and a trigger is still inside — keyboard focus, or a pointer a later edge reports — the tooltip shows again. A tooltip opened by `controller.show()` and clipped away and back behaves as a covered one does: it stays closed unless a trigger is inside. Measured on 4f3db24 (Flutter 3.47.1, widget test): a tooltip shown by Tab focus inside a 200 px `SingleChildScrollView`, scrolled by 300 and back to 0 with focus kept — shown, hidden, and still hidden; the clip-away hide kept show intent set, so the fade's end read it as a close (#126 had recorded this, unadjudicated). Two further calls, asked during #130's run with measurements: **(Q1) a trigger does not show the tooltip while its child is clipped away entirely; it shows once the child is visible, as Covered; `controller.show()` still shows it at the clip edge.** Shown: the first build gated only a tooltip its own clip-away hide had hidden, and two paths it opened left the tooltip at the clip edge (y=64 against a viewport at 100..300) where 4f3db24 and b235b85 kept it hidden (measured by this run on the first, by #130's lens on the second) — the child visible for one frame and clipped again, and the child clipped again during `waitDuration`; and three paths on b235b85 already showed it at the edge — an evicted tooltip whose child was clipped after the eviction's fade, revived; content returning while clipped; a route popped while clipped. The cost shown and accepted: keyboard focus arriving by Tab on a child clipped away under a clip no scroll can undo (a `ClipRect`, say) no longer shows the tooltip at the edge, as it did before; in a scroll view focus traversal scrolls the child into view first. Chosen over gating only after the tooltip's own clip-away hide and hiding again a trigger-shown tooltip found clipped at its first layout, which would draw it at the edge for a frame. **(Q2) Escape or `controller.hide()` while the tooltip waits for its clipped-away child is not a dismissal, as under Covered**: the child coming back shows it. Shown: b235b85 kept it closed, since the clip-away hide itself was a dismissal there; the same sequence under Covered showed it on b235b85; the same holds for an evicted tooltip waiting both for the holder and for its child. Chosen over counting it as a dismissal, as #129 and #136 count an explicit close, which would leave Covered the other way. These are judgements, not derivations; they are the maintainer's to reverse. The implementer's derivations under those calls, not shown to the maintainer: (1) a close already fading out when the child is clipped — a tap, the auto-hide, `controller.hide()` — is still a dismissal, since show intent drops through the same settle every other site uses (#126); (2) a hover-shown tooltip whose child scrolls out from under a resting pointer and back shows again, as it already did on 4f3db24: the clip-away hide runs first, while the pointer still counts as inside, and drops show intent, so nothing is settled; the pointer's exit after the frame ends the wait, and its entry after the frame when the child scrolls back shows it; (3) the wait ends once no trigger is inside, and resumes with the next trigger; (4) a tooltip shown by show intent whose child turns out to be clipped at its first layout hides as a clip-away hide, where `controller.show()` keeps the edge anchor ("a target never had cannot be lost" stays a rule of the controller's show); (5) a child clipped away while a show waits out `waitDuration` calls that wait off, as covering does, so the timer does not show the tooltip at the edge, Escape and `controller.hide()` meanwhile find nothing to cancel (Q2), and a `controller.show()` meanwhile keeps the edge anchor; (6) a child its parent does not paint — kept alive off screen by a lazy list, with a degenerate transform — counts as clipped away.
- **The two existing opt-outs are documented, not changed.** `showDuration` breaks *persistent* and `interactive: false` breaks *hoverable*; their defaults (`null`, `true`) satisfy the criterion, and their dartdoc says so.

The mechanism is the implementer's (no record of the maintainer's): focus inside is read from `FocusManager.primaryFocus`, walking its ancestors to the tooltip, not from a `Focus` widget around the child. A first cut used one, and the code review measured that it took `Focus.of(context)` from every consumer's child, `enableFocus` or not. The `DismissIntent` action sits around the whole tooltip, content included, so focus inside interactive content can reach and consume Escape. Since #113 the registry keeps the record of the tooltips it evicted, beside the one tooltip holding its place, and revives them when that one leaves; before, one app-wide set in the widget's State held every evicted tooltip.

## Consequences

- **Accepted:** a tooltip shown by keyboard focus on a `waitDuration` tooltip waits like a hover does.
- **Accepted:** Escape reaches every tooltip, including ones a consumer meant to stay up; such a consumer re-shows it.
- **Accepted:** Escape on a tooltip that took a focus-held one's place brings the focus-held one back on the same press, because its trigger is still there.
- **Accepted:** the list of shown tooltips and the keyboard handler are app-wide, so with several views an Escape typed in one closes a hovered tooltip in another (read, not run).
- **Not covered:** showing a tooltip from a screen reader's focus — that is [Semantic Tooltip](../../GLOSSARY.md#semantic-tooltip) (ADR-0009), which is heard without opening.
