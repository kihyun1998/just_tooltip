# 0010 — A keyboard user can show and dismiss a tooltip

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-26
- **Context issue:** [#92](https://github.com/kihyun1998/just_tooltip/issues/92) (a focused child does not show its tooltip, and nothing dismisses it with Escape)
- **Amends:** [0003 — Nesting suppression gates a derived hover intent](0003-nesting-suppression-gates-a-derived-hover-intent.md): hover intent becomes [Show Intent](../../GLOSSARY.md#show-intent), with a focus fact and a dismissal term.

## Context

A tooltip showed on hover, on tap and by `controller.show()`, never on focus, and no key closed it. Measured on Flutter 3.41.9 (#92's triage): a `TextButton` child given focus by `requestFocus()` showed nothing; a shown tooltip stayed open through Escape. Flutter's own `Tooltip` does not show on focus either, and neither `raw_tooltip.dart` nor `material/tooltip.dart` handles Escape, so this goes past the prior art and rests on its own reasons.

The yardstick is WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.13, Content on Hover or Focus (w3.org/TR/WCAG21, read 2026-10-26). For content that pointer hover or keyboard focus makes visible, it asks that the content be **dismissible** "without moving pointer hover or keyboard focus", **hoverable**, and **persistent** "until the hover or focus trigger is removed, the user dismisses it, or its information is no longer valid". Its Note 2 names custom tooltips.

## Decision

Maintainer's calls (2026-10-26, grilling on #92's triage):

- **Focus is a core trigger, opt-in: `enableFocus: bool = false`**, beside `enableHover`. A consumer could already call `controller.show()` from `Focus(onFocusChange:)`, but that path bypasses nesting suppression (a programmatic show is not suppressed, ADR-0003) and the trigger-removal hide, which only a core trigger joins. Off by default, so no consumer's keyboard behaviour changes. Chosen over on-by-default and over leaving it to consumers.
- **Focus joins hover in one derived state, renamed Show Intent.** The focus fact sits beside the pointer fact; `waitDuration`, nesting suppression, the content gate and the hide on losing the trigger apply to both from one place. Chosen over a separate path like tap (ADR-0006), which would need suppression rebuilt for it.
- **Only keyboard focus counts** — focus that arrives inside the tooltip while a focus-moving key (Tab, an arrow key) is down; a click, a tap, `requestFocus()`, a Shift-click or focus a closing route gives back does not. Grilling first chose `FocusManager.highlightMode == traditional`, the condition for a focus ring; the completeness read then measured that a mouse click on desktop leaves that mode on, so a click opened the tooltip and kept it until focus left. Shown that, the maintainer chose key-arrived focus (2026-10-26, #92's check).
- **The pointer wins over focus.** A nested tooltip holding focus suppresses its ancestors only while the pointer is not inside them, so a pointer hovering the outer child elsewhere shows the outer tooltip. Measured before the call: the focused inner one kept the outer one silent (maintainer, 2026-10-26, #92's check).
- **A tooltip hidden by the registry comes back.** One whose trigger is still inside when another tooltip takes its place re-derives once no tooltip in its own registry holds the place. Hover never needed it — the pointer cannot be in two children — but focus and the pointer can, and a focus-held tooltip stayed gone (measured; maintainer, 2026-10-26, #92's check).
  - **Amended 2026-11-11 (#113), the maintainer's call.** This read "re-derives when that one hides", and the code re-derived every evicted tooltip whenever any tooltip's show ended. Shown two widget-test repros on efd4042 (Flutter 3.47.1): a chain — A hover-shown, D's `show()` evicts A, B's `show()` evicts D, and when D's fade ends A comes back and dismisses B — and a cross-registry case — A and B in one registry, C in another, B evicts A, and `c.hide()` brings A back over B. The alternative was the literal reading: re-derive when the tooltip that took the place hides, which fixes the cross-registry case and keeps the chain behaviour. The maintainer chose "only once no tooltip in its own registry holds the place": in the chain A comes back when B hides, and a show ending in another registry brings nothing back. A tooltip fading out because it was evicted does not hold the place. This is a judgement, not a derivation; it is the maintainer's to reverse. The call did not cover a tooltip whose registry changes while shown (#114): the maintainer was not shown that this change makes one of its paths worse (the old registry's evicted tooltips wait for another show there to end), so that cost was untested by them, not accepted. #114 then moved a tooltip to its new registry at once (ADR-0007, amended), which closes that path.
  - **Amended 2026-11-12 (#116), the maintainer's call (triage of #116).** #113's rule held for the registry's own revival, but the evicted tooltip's own re-derivations were not gated: one that found its trigger still inside showed it, took the place and dismissed the holder. Measured on 78a9c21 (Flutter 3.47.1, widget tests, one shared registry): A's content lost and regained while evicted → A shown, D hidden; a focus-held F moved by a `GlobalKey` → F shown, H hidden with the pointer on H; the pointer moving from a nested tooltip back onto A → A shown, D hidden. The maintainer chose: while another tooltip holds the place, an evicted tooltip shows again **only on a new trigger act** — the pointer entering its child, or moving onto it from a nested tooltip that was suppressing it (a suppression released by the pointer or keyboard focus moving counts as an act, so the third repro is intended); keyboard focus arriving in it by a key; a tap on it; `controller.show()`. An act shows it as before: it takes the place and dismisses the holder. **Every other re-derivation waits** for the revival, with no visible change meanwhile: its content returning; a suppression released for another reason (the nested tooltip losing its content, leaving the tree, or having `enableHover`/`enableFocus` turned off); `activate` after a `GlobalKey` move with focus still inside; its route becoming current again; `enableHover` or `enableFocus` turned on. When the revival comes, a trigger still inside shows it. This is a judgement, not a derivation; it is the maintainer's to reverse. It did not cover which of several evicted tooltips wins when they are revived together.
    - **The implementer's derivations under that call, not the maintainer's.** (1) "The pointer entering" is a `MouseRegion` edge the pointer's own movement caused. `MouseTracker` also reports edges after a frame, for the tree changing under a resting pointer: a region mounted there gets `onEnter` (so `enableHover` turned on does), a dialog popped from over the child gives one too, and a child moved out from under the pointer gets `onExit` (measured, Flutter 3.47.1). Those come in the post-frame phase and are not acts, which the listed waits for `enableHover` and the route need. The cost the maintainer was not shown: a child scrolled, resized or laid out under a resting pointer, while evicted, waits for the revival too. (2) "Moving onto it from a nested tooltip that was suppressing it" is read as written: a release is an act only when the nested tooltip was suppressing the outer one and the trigger that moved is still inside the outer one afterwards. A release by the pointer leaving the outer one entirely, or by focus leaving a nested tooltip whose suppression the pointer already overrode, is not an act; the first cut counted every release by a moving trigger, and the review and the completeness read both measured an evicted A held by focus coming back, and dismissing the holder, when the pointer went from a nested tooltip straight out of A, where going out from A itself left A waiting. A nested tooltip claiming the outer one is never an act. (3) Show intent an act has set is not dropped by a later re-derivation that is not an act, such as a configuration change during `waitDuration`; it is dropped as before when a term of show intent turns false, and the tooltip then waits again. (4) An evicted tooltip held by both the pointer and focus, one of which leaves, waits: that re-derivation is not on the list of acts.
- **Escape dismisses, on by default.** Dismissal only ever closes, so turning it on cannot break a layout. A hover-shown tooltip's child need not have focus, so the core listens to the keyboard while a tooltip is shown; a consumer has no focus to attach a shortcut to.
  - It closes **the most recently shown** visible tooltip, one per press. More than one can be visible: a host and a tooltip in its content (ADR-0007), or tooltips on separate registries.
  - It **consumes the key only where it can**: when focus is inside the closed tooltip's child, the close runs as the `DismissIntent` action on the focus path, so a dialog behind waits for the next press. A tooltip shown with focus elsewhere — a hover — is closed from a `HardwareKeyboard` handler, and that cannot consume: measured on Flutter 3.41.9, a handler returning `true` still let the dialog's Escape through, so both close. Patching `KeyEventManager.keyMessageHandler` would consume, but it can never be reverted, so it was rejected. Grilling chose "consume only when closed" before this was measured; shown the measurement, the maintainer chose this hybrid over a global handler alone and over the focus path alone, which would leave a hovered tooltip undismissable (2026-10-26, #92's confirm stop).
  - With nothing to close, the focus-path action forwards to the enclosing `DismissIntent` action: a nearer `Actions` for an intent shadows the ancestor's even while disabled (measured), so a tooltip around a dialog's button would otherwise keep the dialog from closing.
  - **One press closes one tooltip.** The keyboard handler runs before the focus path, so it either closes its target or leaves it to the focus path; it never does both. A widget inside the tooltip with its own enabled `DismissIntent` action — a `DropdownMenu` — takes the key first; the tooltip left to the focus path is then closed right after the press, without consuming.
  - With nothing shown, Escape **cancels a show still waiting** out `waitDuration`, and the tooltip stays dismissed until its trigger returns.
  - It closes a tooltip **however it was opened**, `controller.show()` included: the user may always dismiss.
  - A dismissed tooltip stays closed **until its trigger leaves and returns** — the pointer leaves and re-enters the child, or focus leaves and comes back. A tap still toggles it.
- **The two existing opt-outs are documented, not changed.** `showDuration` breaks *persistent* and `interactive: false` breaks *hoverable*; their defaults (`null`, `true`) satisfy the criterion, and their dartdoc says so.

The mechanism is the implementer's (no record of the maintainer's): focus inside is read from `FocusManager.primaryFocus`, walking its ancestors to the tooltip, not from a `Focus` widget around the child. A first cut used one, and the code review measured that it took `Focus.of(context)` from every consumer's child, `enableFocus` or not. The `DismissIntent` action sits around the whole tooltip, content included, so focus inside interactive content can reach and consume Escape. Since #113 the registry keeps the record of the tooltips it evicted, beside the one tooltip holding its place, and revives them when that one leaves; before, one app-wide set in the widget's State held every evicted tooltip.

## Consequences

- **Accepted:** a tooltip shown by keyboard focus on a `waitDuration` tooltip waits like a hover does.
- **Accepted:** Escape reaches every tooltip, including ones a consumer meant to stay up; such a consumer re-shows it.
- **Accepted:** Escape on a tooltip that took a focus-held one's place brings the focus-held one back on the same press, because its trigger is still there.
- **Accepted:** the list of shown tooltips and the keyboard handler are app-wide, so with several views an Escape typed in one closes a hovered tooltip in another (read, not run).
- **Not covered:** showing a tooltip from a screen reader's focus — that is [Semantic Tooltip](../../GLOSSARY.md#semantic-tooltip) (ADR-0009), which is heard without opening.
