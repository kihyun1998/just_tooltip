# 0012 — `waitDuration` delays only a hidden tooltip

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-12
- **Context issue:** [#106](https://github.com/kihyun1998/just_tooltip/issues/106) (a hover that returns during a fade-out with `waitDuration` re-opens the tooltip after the fade, and it stays when the pointer leaves)

## Context

`onChildEnter` started the `waitDuration` timer whatever `isShown` was. A trigger returning while the tooltip faded out therefore waited out the delay; the fade reached `dismissed` during the wait with `_showIntent` set, so `_onAnimationStatus` dropped show intent and marked the tooltip dismissed, but the timer stayed armed. When it fired, `_show` opened a tooltip with no show intent, and the trigger leaving re-derived nothing (`wants == _showIntent`, both false), so it stayed open.

Reproduced on 32be7cc (Flutter 3.47.1, 20 ms pump steps), every one left open after its trigger left: a hover returning during the fade (the issue), the registry reviving an evicted tooltip mid-fade (#106's comment), a nested tooltip releasing its ancestor mid-fade, a tap-close followed by leave and return, keyboard focus returning during the fade, and — with no fade-return at all — `controller.show()` then `hide()` during the wait, and `controller.show()` then eviction during the wait. Escape could not cancel the pending show in that window, and the registry-eviction path of #116 reached the same timer.

Flutter's `RawTooltipState._scheduleShowTooltip` (`widgets/raw_tooltip.dart`, checkout 6655482ec06, read 2026-11-12) delays only when `_controller.isDismissed`; a tooltip animating in, shown or animating out is shown again at once. `_scheduleDismissTooltip` cancels the pending timer on every dismissal, and both assert that the timer is never active while the tooltip animates out.

## Decision

- **While the tooltip is shown — fading out counts — a returning trigger skips `waitDuration` and turns the fade back at once.** The maintainer's call (2026-11-12), chosen for Flutter `RawTooltip` parity over waiting out the rest of the delay after the fade (a lens measured that alternative: the tooltip went off screen for 120 ms between the two shows and called `onShow`/`onHide` twice). This is a judgement, not a derivation; it is the maintainer's to reverse. `TooltipVisibilityScheduler.onChildEnter` reads the `isShown` it is passed (ADR-0001) and arms the timer only when it is false.
- **A controller show cancels a hover show still waiting out `waitDuration`.** The skip alone did not reach the paths with no fade-return: a `controller.show()` during the wait left the timer armed, and when it fired it reopened a tooltip the controller or the registry had hidden meanwhile. `showTooltip` now calls `TooltipVisibilityScheduler.onProgrammaticShow`, which cancels the timer and restarts the auto-hide. With the tap (`onTap` already cancelled it) and the skip, every show now cancels a waiting hover show, so the timer can only fire for a tooltip still hidden. This mechanism is the implementer's call, measured against the two other candidates on the paths listed under Context (#106's run, probes not kept in the repo): resetting the scheduler where show intent drops at `dismissed` and in `dismissTooltip`, and re-checking show intent when the timer fires, each closed those paths too. A lens pass then measured what the first leaves open: the reset at `dismissed` comes only when the hide's fade ends, so a controller hide whose fade outlasted the wait was turned back by the timer (`hide()` 160 ms after a `show()` made during a 300 ms wait: still shown 800 ms later), and `showDuration` counted from the end of the wait (`showDuration` 500 ms: off screen 1000 ms after the show, against 720 ms). Cancelling at the show closes both. It matches `RawTooltipState.ensureTooltipVisible`, its programmatic show, which cancels the pending timer before showing.

## Consequences

- **Accepted by the maintainer (2026-11-12), shown after the call as a case of its own:** a hover entering a tooltip already shown — by `controller.show()`, say — restarts `showDuration` at the entry instead of after `waitDuration`.
- **Accepted by the maintainer (2026-11-12), shown with the alternative — the timer turning the controller's hide back:** a hover that started waiting before a `controller.show()` does not show the tooltip again when the controller hides it; the pointer leaves and returns first, as after any close while the trigger is inside (and as a tooltip with no `waitDuration` already behaved, read not run).
- **Accepted by the maintainer (2026-11-12):** a tooltip the registry brings back after its fade has ended is hidden, so it waits out `waitDuration` again though the pointer never left (a 20 ms fade on the tooltip that took its place: back 340 ms after that one's `hide()`, measured by #106's lens, same before this change). A revival is not counted as a trigger still inside a shown tooltip.
- **Not covered:** the eviction gate in show intent for #116 beyond its stale-timer path, a closed tooltip revived when evicted during its closing fade (#117), and a zero-length fade's `onHide` inside a registry show (#118). The maintainer's call covered the trigger returning while shown; it did not judge those neighbours.
