# 0012 — `waitDuration` delays only a hidden tooltip

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-12
- **Context issue:** [#106](https://github.com/kihyun1998/just_tooltip/issues/106) (a hover that returns during a fade-out with `waitDuration` re-opens the tooltip after the fade, and it stays when the pointer leaves)

## Context

`onChildEnter` started the `waitDuration` timer whatever `isShown` was. A trigger returning while the tooltip faded out therefore waited out the delay; the fade reached `dismissed` during the wait with `_showIntent` set, so `_onAnimationStatus` dropped show intent and marked the tooltip dismissed, but the timer stayed armed. When it fired, `_show` opened a tooltip with no show intent, and the trigger leaving re-derived nothing (`wants == _showIntent`, both false), so it stayed open.

Reproduced on 32be7cc (Flutter 3.47.1, 20 ms pump steps), every one left open after its trigger left: a hover returning during the fade (the issue), the registry reviving an evicted tooltip mid-fade (#106's comment), a nested tooltip releasing its ancestor mid-fade, a tap-close followed by leave and return, keyboard focus returning during the fade, and — with no fade-return at all — `controller.show()` then `hide()` during the wait, and `controller.show()` then eviction during the wait. Escape could not cancel the pending show in that window, and the registry-eviction path of #116 reached the same timer.

Flutter's `RawTooltipState._scheduleShowTooltip` (`widgets/raw_tooltip.dart`, checkout 6655482ec06, read 2026-11-12) delays only when `_controller.isDismissed`; a tooltip animating in, shown or animating out is shown again at once. `_scheduleDismissTooltip` cancels the pending timer on every dismissal, and both assert that the timer is never active while the tooltip animates out.

## Decision

- **While the tooltip is shown — fading out counts — a returning trigger skips `waitDuration` and turns the fade back at once.** The maintainer's call (2026-11-12), chosen for Flutter `RawTooltip` parity over waiting out the rest of the delay after the fade (a lens measured that alternative: the tooltip went off screen for 120 ms between the two shows and called `onShow`/`onHide` twice). This is a judgement, not a derivation; it is the maintainer's to reverse. `TooltipVisibilityScheduler.onChildEnter` reads the `isShown` it is passed (ADR-0001) and arms the timer only when it is false.
- **A controller show cancels a hover show still waiting out `waitDuration`.** The skip alone did not reach the paths with no fade-return: a `controller.show()` during the wait left the timer armed, and when it fired it reopened a tooltip the controller or the registry had hidden meanwhile. `showTooltip` now calls `TooltipVisibilityScheduler.onProgrammaticShow`, which cancels the timer and restarts the auto-hide. With the tap (`onTap` already cancelled it) and the skip, every show now cancels a waiting hover show, so the timer can only fire for a tooltip still hidden. This mechanism is the implementer's call, measured against the two other candidates on the paths listed under Context (#106's run, probes not kept in the repo): resetting the scheduler where show intent drops at `dismissed` and in `dismissTooltip`, and re-checking show intent when the timer fires, each closed those paths too. A lens pass then measured what the first leaves open: the reset at `dismissed` comes only when the hide's fade ends, so a controller hide whose fade outlasted the wait was turned back by the timer (`hide()` 160 ms after a `show()` made during a 300 ms wait: still shown 800 ms later), and `showDuration` counted from the end of the wait (`showDuration` 500 ms: off screen 1000 ms after the show, against 720 ms). Cancelling at the show closes both. It matches `RawTooltipState.ensureTooltipVisible`, its programmatic show, which cancels the pending timer before showing.

## Consequences

- **Accepted by the maintainer (2026-11-12), shown after the call as a case of its own:** a hover entering a tooltip already shown — by `controller.show()`, say — restarts `showDuration` at the entry instead of after `waitDuration`.
- **Accepted by the maintainer (2026-11-12), shown with the alternative — the timer turning the controller's hide back:** a hover that started waiting before a `controller.show()` does not show the tooltip again when the controller hides it; the pointer leaves and returns first, as after any close while the trigger is inside (and as a tooltip with no `waitDuration` already behaved, read not run).
- **Accepted by the maintainer (2026-11-12):** a tooltip the registry brings back after its fade has ended is hidden, so it waits out `waitDuration` again though the pointer never left (a 20 ms fade on the tooltip that took its place: back 340 ms after that one's `hide()`, measured by #106's lens, same before this change). A revival is not counted as a trigger still inside a shown tooltip.
- **Not covered:** the eviction gate in show intent for #116 beyond its stale-timer path, a closed tooltip revived when evicted during its closing fade (#117), and a zero-length fade's `onHide` inside a registry show (#118; since fixed, an implementer's derivation: the evicting tooltip registers last in `_show`, so a show from that `onHide` takes the place, [visibility-lifecycle](../map/territory/visibility-lifecycle.md)). The maintainer's call covered the trigger returning while shown; it did not judge those neighbours.

## Amendment — 2026-11-16: the body of an interactive tooltip holds it as its child does

- **Context issues:** [#133](https://github.com/kihyun1998/just_tooltip/issues/133) (a `controller.show()` while the pointer rests on the body restarts the paused `showDuration`), [#134](https://github.com/kihyun1998/just_tooltip/issues/134) (the pointer entering the body during a fade does not turn it back), [#147](https://github.com/kihyun1998/just_tooltip/issues/147) (with `enableHover` off, crossing the body stops the auto-hide for good).
- **Decided by the maintainer (2026-11-16, triage of #133/#134), a judgement, theirs to reverse:** the pointer on an interactive tooltip's body holds it as the pointer on its child does, for Flutter `RawTooltip` parity: in `widgets/raw_tooltip.dart` (checkout 6655482ec06) the overlay's `_ExclusiveMouseRegion` calls the same `_handleMouseEnter` / `_handleMouseExit` as the child's, and `_scheduleShowTooltip` turns a fade back. Shown with two alternatives, not chosen: fix #133 only and close #134; keep both as they were.
- **What it says, with what each issue measured on fd0a9be (Flutter 3.47.1, 20 ms pump steps):**
  - The body's pause and resume pair up whatever `enableHover` is (#147). With hover off, `onTooltipExit` returned before resuming, so a tap- or controller-opened tooltip whose body the pointer crossed never auto-hid again (still shown 2 s later). #147's run found the same with `interactive` turned off under the resting pointer, whose exit arrives with `interactive` false (shown 3 s later); the resume now reads neither.
  - While the pointer rests on the body, no show starts `showDuration`; the pointer leaving the body starts it (#133). A second `controller.show()` with the pointer on the body hid the tooltip under it 800 ms later. The pause is now a fact the State keeps (`_pointerOnBody`) and passes to every scheduler call that starts the countdown, not the absence of a timer the next show re-armed. Where the fact lives is the implementer's call ([visibility-scheduling](../map/territory/visibility-scheduling.md)).
  - With `interactive` and `enableHover`, the pointer entering the body while the tooltip fades out does what the pointer entering the child does in that same state, under the same dismissal and eviction rules, whatever began the fade (#134). The bridge running out with the pointer reaching the body mid-fade left it gone, `[show, hide]`, where reaching the child turned it back, `[show]`; the same for an auto-hide fade the pointer crossed from the child onto the body. Now the body's `onEnter` asks the State the question the reconcile would answer for a child entry, and a yes requests a show, which turns the fade back without a second `onShow` (the mechanism is the implementer's call, [show-intent](../map/territory/show-intent.md)). With `enableHover` off, it does not turn the fade back: turning a fade back from the body is a hover show.
- **Consequences, measured in #134's run with child and body side by side (`test/just_tooltip_body_test.dart`):** a fade begun by `controller.hide()` or by Escape with the pointer away is turned back from the body, as from the child; a tooltip the registry evicted is turned back and takes the registry back, the tooltip that took its place hiding; the body or the child coming under a still pointer turns back a `controller.hide()` fade but not an eviction fade (no new act); Escape while keyboard focus is inside, and an auto-hide fade during which focus still gives show intent, are turned back by neither; a fade begun by the child scrolling out of sight is not turned back from the body. The turned-back tooltip has the show intent it would have had after crossing from the child onto the body without a fade — none, since the pointer is on the body, not the child, unless keyboard focus holds it — so the pointer leaving the body arms the bridge, restarts the auto-hide, or, while focus holds it, does nothing, and the closers that end a tooltip crossed onto from the child end it too (an implementer's derivation, corrected in #134's closers run: a turned-back tooltip focus held after an eviction fade had no show intent, and focus leaving, among other closers, left it shown for good; [show-intent](../map/territory/show-intent.md)).
- **Not shown to the maintainer, an implementer's reading of "as the child does":** that Escape with the pointer away is turned back from the body follows from Escape's dismissal holding only a trigger inside at the close (ADR-0010); the brief named Escape among the fades the rule covers.
- **The body turns a fade back only on entry** (maintainer's call, 2026-11-16, asked from #134's run, a judgement, theirs to reverse). A revival during an eviction fade, with the pointer resting on the body, does not turn the tooltip back; resting on the child, it does. Measured in #134's run: A shown by hover, the pointer resting on A, `b.show()`, `b.hide()` 60 ms later — on the child `[show A, show B, hide B]`, A shown; on the body `[show A, show B, hide B, hide A]`, as on fd0a9be. Shown with the alternative, a child-like turn-back on revival, and its cost: a second path in the reconcile reading the body fact as a trigger for turning a fade back, with tests and a record, while a revival after the fade has ended would still not bring the tooltip back, since it has no body then.
- **Leaving a body that stopped taking the pointer hides at once** (maintainer's call, 2026-11-16, asked from #148's triage, a judgement, theirs to reverse). With `enableHover` on, no `showDuration` and keyboard focus not holding it, the body's exit that arrives with `interactive` off — `interactive` turned off under a resting pointer — hides the tooltip at once, as a non-interactive tooltip hides on its child's exit. It used to arm nothing, and a hover-shown tooltip, whose show intent had dropped at the child's exit, stayed shown for good (measured on fd0a9be and ada5e3e: shown 3 s after the change, and 1 s after the pointer left). The pointer going on to the child turns the fade back, as above. Shown with the alternative, hiding after the bridge, not chosen. Not covered: two mice, the `showDuration` case (#147); `enableHover` off is the next bullet.
- **Leaving a body entered with hover on is a hover exit, though `enableHover` has been turned off since** ([#150](https://github.com/kihyun1998/just_tooltip/issues/150); maintainer's call, 2026-11-17, a judgement, theirs to reverse). With no `showDuration` and keyboard focus not holding it, the body's exit that follows an entry made while `enableHover` was on is handled as a hover exit, whatever showed the tooltip; nothing happens when `enableHover` turns off, so the tooltip's lifetime is what it would have been with hover still on. It used to arm nothing: a tooltip with no show intent and the pointer resting on its body stayed shown for good (measured by #133's run on fd0a9be, and reproduced as a failing test on bd8de03). Shown with the alternative, hiding when `enableHover` turns off, not chosen. **That exit hides at once rather than after the bridge** (an implementer's derivation; it falls to a better one): the bridge exists so the cursor can cross onto a hover trigger, and with `enableHover` off the child has no `MouseRegion` to cross to; a body re-entered while the bridge waited would also have been entered with hover off, so its exit would arm nothing and the tooltip would stay shown for good again. It matches the bullet above, and `interactive` turned off together with `enableHover` hides at once too. A body entered while `enableHover` was already off still arms nothing on exit (the pointer is not a trigger then); `enableHover` turned back on before the exit arms the bridge as before. Pinned in `test/just_tooltip_body_test.dart` (group *enableHover turned off with the pointer on the body*): shown by hover, by a tap or by `controller.show()`; keyboard focus holding it (kept); `showDuration` set (restarted on leaving, unchanged); `interactive` off too; leaving during a `controller.hide()` fade; the pointer coming back onto the body during the fade (not turned back, as with hover off); hover on again before the exit; an eviction, with the pointer staying or leaving during its fade (gone, and not revived when the other tooltip hides). Not covered: two mice (one entry fact per tooltip, as `_pointerOnBody`) and the `showDuration` set afterwards case (#151, next bullet). Two neighbours this bullet left open are decided in the bullet after next: a pointer resting on the body holding the tooltip against another trigger leaving (#149), and a Hover Bridge already armed when the pointer enters the body with `enableHover` off (#154, found by #150's completeness read).
- **A `showDuration` change on a shown tooltip applies at once** ([#151](https://github.com/kihyun1998/just_tooltip/issues/151); maintainer's call, 2026-11-17, a judgement, theirs to reverse, chosen over applying it from the next show): the countdown restarts from the change, paused while the pointer rests on the body, as a shown tooltip follows its widget's current configuration ([live-configuration](../map/territory/live-configuration.md)). Set on a tooltip shown with none, it used to start nothing: `onChildExit` leaves the hide to the countdown, `startAutoHide` ran only on a show, and once the pointer or keyboard focus left, the tooltip stayed shown for good (measured by #133's run; reproduced as a failing test on 8e9a797). How each case resolves is the implementer's derivation from the existing rules; each falls to a better one:
  - **Set or changed:** `TooltipVisibilityScheduler.onShowDurationChange` calls `startAutoHide` with the new value, whoever showed the tooltip. A trigger still inside does not stop the countdown, so it closes the tooltip as an auto-hide does, and the tooltip stays closed until the trigger leaves and returns. A Hover Bridge already armed stays: the exit was taken under the old value and its hide is already under way.
  - **Cleared:** the countdown is cancelled, and the tooltip ends where the new value puts it. A trigger exit made during this show and left to the countdown — the child's exit, by the pointer or keyboard focus, or a hover exit from the body, with `showDuration` set, focus not holding the tooltip, and no trigger back and no `controller.show()` since (`_JustTooltipState._exitLeftToCountdown`) — asks for the hide that exit asks for with no `showDuration`: with the pointer off the body, at once through `onChildExit`'s rule (the bridge when `interactive`, else the hide); with the pointer on the body, at the body's exit, whatever `enableHover` is, since that is where the countdown it replaces would have resumed. The fact stays for the rest of the show, so a body re-entered during that bridge asks again on its exit. With no such exit — a `controller.show()` or a touch tap, a trigger or focus still holding it, a body crossed with hover off (#147) — the tooltip stays, closed by what closes a tooltip shown with none.
  - **During a fade-out, or on a hidden tooltip:** nothing. A fade turned back starts the countdown by the current value (`onChildEnter`, `onProgrammaticShow`) or pauses it (the body). A countdown armed during an eviction fade would close the evicted tooltip while it waits and cancel its revival (mutation measured).
  - The fact lives in the State, not the scheduler, which keeps no state but its timers (ADR-0001); the scheduler takes it per call, as `pointerOnBody`.
  - Pinned in `test/just_tooltip_live_config_test.dart` (group *showDuration changed on a shown tooltip*): set on a tooltip shown by hover (pointer leaving, and resting), focus, `controller.show()` and a touch tap; the pointer on the body; during a bridge; changed shorter and longer; cleared on a `controller.show()`, a touch tap, a resting hover, a hover or focus already gone, the pointer crossed onto the body, focus gone with the pointer on a body entered with hover off, the trigger back, a `controller.show()` after the hover left, a tap after a hover show the countdown ended, a body crossed with hover on and off, focus holding it; during an auto-hide, bridge and eviction fade; set on a hidden tooltip. The scheduler's half in `test/tooltip_visibility_scheduler_test.dart`. Not covered: two mice and #143 (#149, and the bridge-before-hover-off-body-entry hole this change had left as it was, are decided in the next bullet); a hover show still waiting out `waitDuration` when the value changes, which kept the value of when its wait began and, set during the wait, ended shown for good once the pointer left (same on 8e9a797, measured) — since fixed by #155: the show reads the value when the wait runs out ([visibility-scheduling](../map/territory/visibility-scheduling.md)); a value changing on every rebuild, which restarts the countdown each time. **Re-confirmed with the prior art shown** (maintainer, 2026-11-17, a judgement, theirs to reverse): Flutter's `RawTooltip` (3.41.9) has no `didUpdateWidget` and reads `touchDelay` and `dismissDelay` only when it arms a timer, so the alternative not chosen, applying the value from the next show, is Flutter's behaviour, and the earlier calls in this record were made for `RawTooltip` parity. That divergence was not shown when the call was first made; it was shown afterwards, together with the cost of a value that changes on every rebuild (a `Duration` compares by value, so only a value that actually differs restarts the countdown), and the call was kept.
- **A pointer resting on the body, entered with `enableHover` on, holds the tooltip against the Hover Bridge another trigger leaving would arm; a body entered with `enableHover` off cancels no bridge** ([#149](https://github.com/kihyun1998/just_tooltip/issues/149), [#154](https://github.com/kihyun1998/just_tooltip/issues/154); maintainer's calls, 2026-11-17, during triage of both, judgements, theirs to reverse).
  - **The hold (#149).** While the pointer rests on the body having entered it with `enableHover` on, another trigger leaving — keyboard focus leaving, `enableFocus` turned off, any re-derivation that takes the scheduler's child-exit path (`onChildExit`) — arms no bridge. The body's own exit then asks for the hide by the rules above: the bridge with `enableHover` on, at once with it turned off since the entry (#150). The body holds against the bridge as it already holds against the `showDuration` countdown. It used to hide after the bridge under the resting pointer, where resting on the child kept it (confirmed on bb75909 by the body test this change flips). **Show Intent's definition does not change**: chosen over making the body pointer a term of show intent, which would reach nesting suppression (ADR-0003), eviction and revival, and dismissal (ADR-0010).
  - **No cancel without hover (#154).** `onTooltipEnter` cancels an armed bridge only for an entry made with `enableHover` on; it still pauses the `showDuration` countdown whatever `enableHover` is (#147). A bridge armed before such an entry — by the child's exit when `enableHover` turned off under the pointer, or by keyboard focus leaving a tooltip with hover off — runs out and hides the tooltip under the pointer. The entry used to cancel it, and the exit of a body entered with hover off arms nothing (#150), so the tooltip stayed shown for good (reproduced by triage on bb75909: hover-shown, `enableHover: false`, the pointer onto the body within 20 ms and away 200 ms later — still shown 2 s later, `[show]`). Chosen over holding with hover off too and re-arming the bridge on the exit.
  - **Derived from those** (implementer's derivations; each falls to a better one). The hover state at the body's entry decides, read from `_bodyEnteredWithHover` as in #150: a body entered with hover on keeps holding after `enableHover` turns off, and its exit hides at once; a body entered with hover off holds nothing, so keyboard focus leaving under it arms the bridge as before. The hold changes only `onChildExit`: what hides without the bridge — an eviction, Escape, `controller.hide()`, the child clipped away, Covered, content loss — still hides a tooltip the body holds, and none of them brings it back. With `showDuration` set the countdown owns the hide, unchanged. The fact goes to the scheduler per call (`hoverOnBody`), as `pointerOnBody` does (ADR-0001). The `showDuration` guard on the child exit's `_exitLeftToCountdown` reddens tests when removed — two of #150's and the new *with enableHover turned off since the entry …* — since without it a body exit after `enableHover` turned off arms the bridge instead of hiding at once (measured by #149's run; #151's record of it surviving every test predates #150's tests).
  - **A body that appears under a still pointer is entered** (maintainer's call, 2026-11-17, asked from #149's run, a judgement, theirs to reverse). The body's `onEnter` after a frame — the tooltip shown by Tab where the cursor already rests — records the entry as a moving pointer's does, so that body holds too: the same rule as #150's entry fact and as the child, whose region mounted under a resting pointer counts as the pointer inside. Measured by #149's lens and run: focus leaving then left it shown (`[show]`), where bb75909 hid it (`[show, hide]`). Shown with the alternative, holding only for a pointer that moved onto the body (as #134's turn-back distinguishes), not chosen.
  - **A focus suppressor does not hide a tooltip whose body the pointer rests on** (maintainer's call, 2026-11-17, triage of #156 and its run, a judgement, theirs to reverse). The pointer resting on the body, entered with `enableHover` on, counts as the pointer, and the pointer wins over a focus suppressor: `_suppressedWith` counts a focus suppressor only while neither the pointer is inside the child with `enableHover` on (ADR-0010) nor `_pointerOnBody && _bodyEnteredWithHover` holds. It holds in every order. Show Intent's definition does not change; only what suppresses it does.
    - **The triage premise, superseded.** #156's triage first decided this as documentation only ("no runtime code change"), on two measured paths where A had no show intent left (A hover-shown, the pointer onto its body, D focused; A Tab-shown, the pointer onto its body, focus away, Shift+Tab onto D): suppression hides only through show intent's transition to `false`, so a body-held tooltip with none left already stayed, `[show A, show D]`. (On bb75909 the focus path gave `[show A, hide A, show D]` because the bridge hid A after focus left — the defect #149 fixed — not because of suppression.) The run's lens then found a path the triage was not shown: A with `enableFocus` and a focusable of its own, Tab onto it, the pointer onto A's body, Tab straight onto D — focus never leaves A, so A keeps show intent, D's focus suppression dropped it and `_reconcileShowIntent` hid A, `[show A, show D, hide A]`, the same as with no pointer on the body. Shown that measurement, the maintainer chose the runtime fix over narrowing the rule to body-held tooltips with no show intent (that path left as a known hole) and over deciding that path's hide.
    - **Derived from that call** (implementer's derivations; each falls to a better one). The body's `onExit` schedules a reconcile while a focus suppressor is present, so a tooltip shown by focus still inside it drops show intent and hides at once when the pointer leaves the body, as a suppressed tooltip does when the pointer leaves its child; without it show intent stayed set through the bridge's hide, and focus back on A's own focusable did not show A again (measured by mutation). With a trigger still inside holding show intent and no `showDuration`, that exit asks the scheduler for no hide either: the pointer moving back onto the child changes nothing the child's reconcile acts on, so the bridge the exit armed stayed armed, and A hid and stayed dismissed (measured by the run's second lens). A focus release decides whether it is a trigger act for the ancestor (`_claimAncestors`) from the ancestor's suppression without the body pointer, so in one registry focus back from D during A's eviction fade turns the fade back, as before the body counted (the second lens measured a hide and a re-show otherwise). The crossing from A's child onto its body with D already focused no longer hides A for the turn-back to bring back within the same event (before: `_hide` then `_turnsBackFromBody`, no `onHide`; traced by #156's run).
    - Pinned in `test/just_tooltip_nested_test.dart` (group *a tooltip the body holds, and a nested tooltip focused*): the two triage paths (the hover one also leaving the body, which lets A go), the straight Tab onto D (red without the fix), leaving the body (A hides at once) and focus coming back, the pointer back from the body onto the child, one registry with focus back during A's eviction fade, the crossing with D already focused, and a body entered with `enableHover` off, which does not count. Shown with the alternative, hiding a body-held tooltip when suppression begins, which needs a new fact telling a body hold from a `controller.show()` (exempt under ADR-0003); not chosen. Not covered: pointer suppressors (not separately checked); one registry, where D's show evicts A (registry policy, unchanged).
  - Pinned in `test/just_tooltip_body_test.dart` (groups *a pointer resting on the body, entered with enableHover on, holds the tooltip against another trigger leaving* and *a body entered with enableHover off holds nothing against the Hover Bridge*; the flipped tests in *a tooltip keyboard focus holds, its eviction fade turned back from the body …*) and `test/tooltip_visibility_scheduler_test.dart`. Not covered: two mice; #155 (since fixed, [visibility-scheduling](../map/territory/visibility-scheduling.md)).
